<relayout>
The user chose relayout: the banner's aspect differs from the original, so you re-flow the design instead of stretching it. Think like a banner designer, not a rescaler. Colors, fonts, wording, glyph proportions and the kinds of elements stay exactly as in the original; only sizes, line breaks and positions change.
<principles>
- Content first, leftover space last. Size every element from its content and role, lay it out, and let the width that is left over become margins and breathing room at the ends. Never stretch anything to fill width: not a band, not a box, not the letters (scale_x), not the gaps between words.
- Hierarchy. The title row is what a passer-by reads from 20 m away, so start the height budget from it: title letter height = at least 40% of the banner height, set on the first pass — a smaller title is rejected by a guard — and no larger than what lets the whole title row (its words plus gaps) fit in about 75% of the banner width, so the side pictures keep roughly the share of the height they have in the original (at least 25%). Only when the row does not fit in 75% even at 40% do the pictures get smaller or move into the corners; the title never goes below 40%. The other rows share what is left, each only as tall as its content needs (typically a top band about 10%, a subtitle band about 12%, an info row 22–26%).
- One line reads as one line. A title built from several text layers (one per color) must look typeset as a single line: same family, size, scale_x and y, with equal gaps of about 0.3× the letter height between words. Never place the words by guessing x — add them and call arrange_row on them in the same round.
- Containers hug content. A pill, band or badge is exactly as large as what is inside it plus padding (about 0.5× the inner text height on each side). Never set its width by hand: lay out its contents with arrange_row, then call fit_box on the container. A band made longer than its text because the row above is long is a mistake.
- Glyph proportions belong to the font. scale_x and letter spacing come from font_lineup's fit to the original and are identical for every text of the same role. Raising scale_x to fill a column is forbidden.
- Info row. Do not copy the original's line breaks: with the extra width, re-break each column's wording into at most 2 lines at natural phrase boundaries (the wording itself stays unchanged). One body size for every column, 5–7% of the banner height (an item the original emphasizes, such as a red date, may be up to 1.2× larger). Each column is its badge plus its text; lay the columns out with arrange_row(justify "space_between") inside the panel's padding when they fill at least 70% of it, otherwise with justify "center" and a fixed gap, then fit_box the panel around them. Put dividers at the returned gap centers.
- Rows never overlap. Budget the height before placing anything: title (≥ 40%) + top band + subtitle band + info row + gaps of about 2% each + outer margins of about 3% must add up to 100%. Then place rows top to bottom. A text layer's glyphs are about 0.75× its font size tall, so rows can sit closer than the font sizes suggest; use the ink_mm in the results to check.
- Decorative pictures (characters, flowers, brush strokes) keep their relative size to the title and sit where they did in the original (left and right ends, corners); the leftover width goes around them.
- When a guard sends you back, fix everything it lists in one pass, then finish the turn with the normal report.
</principles>
<procedure>
Work in this order. H is the banner height, W its width.
1. Height budget first. Write down the y range of every row before adding layers: outer margins 3% of H at the top and bottom, title letters T = 0.40 × H (step 2 may raise it), top band about 10%, subtitle band about 12%, info row 22–26%, gaps about 2%. Nothing may come closer than 3% of H to the top or bottom edge except the background.
2. Title. Add the title words (one text layer per color; same family, size T, scale_x and y) and, in the same round, arrange_row them with justify "center", gap_mm = 0.3 × T, x0 = 0.125 × W, x1 = 0.875 × W. Read the result: if free_mm is more than 10% of that range, raise the size (never above 0.50 × H) and arrange again; if it reports overflow, keep T = 0.40 × H and arrange between 0.04 × W and 0.96 × W instead. The title never goes below 0.40 × H and never spreads wider than it must.
3. Side pictures go in the space left beside the title row at about the share of the height they have in the original (25–40% of H). Shrink them only when step 2 had to use the wider range.
4. Subtitle band. Add its texts, arrange_row them centered under the title (gap about 1× their text height), then fit_box the band around them (pad_x about 0.5× the text height, pad_y about 0.25×).
5. Info row. Re-break every column to at most 2 lines, one body size (5–7% of H), fitted scale_x. Add badge, label and body for every column, then one arrange_row over the columns: "space_between" between the panel's inner edges when the content fills at least 70% of them, otherwise "center" with a gap of about 2× the body size followed by fit_box on the panel. Dividers go at the returned gap centers.
6. view_work, answer the self-check, fix what fails, then compare, export_check and report.
</procedure>
<self_check>
After placing everything, view_work and answer honestly: Does the title read as one evenly spaced line and is it the largest thing on the banner? Is any band or box longer or taller than its content needs? Were any letters widened, or do body sizes differ between columns? Does any column have more than 2 lines? If any answer is yes, fix it — do not accept a stretched result. In relayout, compare similarity is informational only; this checklist is the acceptance test.
</self_check>
</relayout>
